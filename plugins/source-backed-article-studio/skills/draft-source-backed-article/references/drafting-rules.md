# Drafting rules

## Evidence priority

1. User-confirmed facts and authoritative primary sources
2. Direct experience records
3. Dated observations attributed to their source
4. Background material used only for context
5. Style samples used only for voice and structure

Conflicts must be surfaced for review, not silently resolved. A newer source does not automatically override an authoritative source.

For a source marked `publishable: false`, reader-facing prose may use only an exact statement from its `allowed_claims`. The draft must not identify or cite that source. If useful wording would broaden the approved statement, leave it for review instead.

## Writing

- Lead with what the reader will learn.
- Translate implementation details into what changed for the user.
- Label plans, hypotheses, and unverified observations clearly.
- Prefer concrete limitations to vague disclaimers.
- Keep technical identifiers out of reader-facing prose unless understanding them is necessary.
- Do not imitate distinctive passages from a style source. Use only high-level characteristics.

## Links and promotions

Use only destinations recorded in a publishable source and confirmed to exist. If no destination exists, omit the link and leave a review item. Mark advertising or affiliate relationships for human confirmation; do not assume that every citation is promotional.
